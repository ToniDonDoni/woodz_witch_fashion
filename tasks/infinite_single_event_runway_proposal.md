# Infinite Single-Event Runway — Concept Proposal

**Status:** reviewed design-only proposal; no runtime, sprite, website, or release changes.
**Review update:** 2026-10-08, incorporating the five implementation and art-direction suggestions from the PR #11 concept review.

## North star

A woman walks continuously through absolute black. Occasionally **exactly one** neon chalk/pencil apparition emerges, performs a small evocative action, and vanishes. A quiet black interval follows. Every apparition should feel like a newly discovered being or phenomenon, not a preset animation replayed with a different color.

**Non-negotiables**
1. At most **one active visual event** at any instant, including entrances, exits, trails, particles, and lingering glow. The walker is the only persistent visual subject.
2. New events are deterministic functions of **(dream seed, event index)**, not frame count, rendering speed, or previous draws.
3. A single local offline self-contained HTML page, no live model calls or runtime network requests.
4. Preserve the existing 33 footage-derived walking frames, sprite cadence, scale, position and existing gait seam; do not repaint the model.
5. The stage remains black between events. No permanent moon, forest, stars, decorative dust, captions, or background scenery.
6. Style: black negative space, neon chalk/crayon/pencil contour, restrained luminous halo, 12 Hz intentional hand-drawn contour boil, smooth RAF translation/fades.

## What “infinite” means (and does not mean)

The scheduler supports an **unbounded indexed stream** of events: event 0, 1, 2, ... generated on demand, without a fixed catalog or stored sequence. Each event is composed from a parameterized **visual grammar** with continuous-valued geometry, motion, pose, timing and palette. The number of possible parameter combinations is enormous, but finite computer precision, a finite grammar and repeated motifs mean **perceptual uniqueness cannot be mathematically guaranteed**. Do not market this as infinitely many genuinely different species.

Our design goal is *high perceived novelty over long viewing sessions*, not merely different random seeds. Define a reviewable novelty metric and test it with human observers.


## Visual grammar: make creatures, not a list of stickers

The grammar is built from **curated silhouette families with controlled internal variability**, not random appendage numbers attached to a random body. The goal is legible illustrated creatures whose personality emerges in a **micro-encounter with the walker**.

Start with three explicit construction systems:

1. **Winged apparition** — a recognizable paired-wing envelope from one thorax spine, structurally symmetric at rest but with seeded vein branching, wing-profile curvature, notch count and one intentional asymmetry. Actions: flutter closer, momentarily hover beside the walker, hesitate, then fly away or dissolve to a chalk stroke. Eyes are optional; no unrelated quadruped limbs.
2. **Watchful / eyed presence** — a deliberate silhouette with one to three mass shapes, a coherent head/torso relation and paired or single eye anchors. The outlines can suggest a wolf, fox, owl or unknown creature without becoming a disconnected line soup. Actions: peek from one side, turn or blink, notice the walker, **briefly synchronize with one of her steps**, then retreat. Its gaze timing tracks the walker, but the presence must not cover her.
3. **Abstract ribbon spirit** — a continuous spline with taper, occasional branch tips, sparse cavities and at most one face-like feature. Actions: coil, unfold, follow her motion for one beat, bend toward her, collapse into a single glowing pencil line. Do not scatter arbitrary disconnected strokes.

Each constructor enforces geometry constraints: connected silhouettes, bounded appendage count, coherent joint anchors, minimum negative-space size, reasonable screen silhouette occupancy, contrast limits and compatible actions. Visual variants are generated by **continuous morphology parameters** within those constraints, not by arbitrary element count or recoloring. Reject malformed or unrecognizable candidates deterministically.

Five compositional layers then refine each encounter:

1. **Topology** — family-specific connected structure, envelope, appendage attachments and silhouette cavities.
2. **Morphology** — seeded proportions, bend angles, controlled asymmetry, detail density and hatching.
3. **Action / micro-story** — *notice → react → brief relationship with the walker → depart*. Actions must be compatible with the topology, and their timing must be readable at phone scale.
4. **Choreography** — restrained entrance side, depth, distance from the figure, gaze or step synchronization, clear exit. Do not tint or hide the figure.
5. **Material** — chalk/pencil line stacks, sparse glowing contours and a restrained neon palette. A shape must remain legible even with glow turned off.

Distinctiveness should come from the *interaction* and shape, not solely color/scale/rotation. The same family can produce genuinely different micro-stories without promising infinite semantic uniqueness.

## Grammar with controlled coherence

Use a small number of **structural archetype constructors**, each producing many distinct topologies and compatible actions. This is a practical trade-off: unconstrained random geometry is easy to generate but usually looks meaningless.

Pseudo-interface:

```ts
type EventSpec = {
  id: number;
  seed: number;
  silhouette: ShapeGraph;
  action: ActionTimeline;
  path: MotionPath;
  material: ChalkMaterial;
  start: number;
  duration: number;
};

spec = generateEvent(dreamSeed, eventIndex); // pure, stable
renderEvent(ctx, spec, simulationTime, viewport);
```

An event is an *immutable specification* of geometry and motion. Its appearance at time `t` is derived from that spec and `t`, not from accumulated simulation frames. Geometry should be regenerated from spec for deterministic seeks; heavy raster art may be cached while active.


## Exactly-one scheduler

The woman always walks. The event scheduler owns **one visual entity and all its traces/particles/glow** from first reveal until complete disappearance:

```text
BLACK_GAP -> REVEAL -> ENCOUNTER -> DEPART -> BLACK_GAP -> ...
```

**First implementation uses O(1) independently indexed fixed-length slots** for reproducible seeking, not a variable-duration chain of previous event schedules.

- Proposed slot duration `S=7.0 s` (art-direction hypothesis, not a fixed creative requirement).
- For non-empty slot `n`, derive its leading gap `lead(n)` from seed/index in `[0.35, 0.95]` seconds, and its entire event lifecycle `duration(n)` in `[4.4, 4.9]` seconds. Define `tail(n)=S-lead(n)-duration(n)`, which is guaranteed >= **1.15 s**. Therefore `lead+duration+tail=S` and **no event is clipped**. The event duration includes reveal, action, disappearance and final glow decay; no remnants survive into the tail.
- Adjacent-event actual black gap is `tail(n)+lead(n+1)`, approximately 1.5–3.2 s under these bounds. Include **occasional fully empty slots** generated by a deterministic rare-event rule, yielding longer intentional silences.
- Every non-empty seven-second slot yields around 8–9 encounters per minute. The immediate artistic study should contain **one minute and about 8–10 events**; empty slots may reduce the count. These intervals are *starting points for live viewing*, not UX acceptance limits.
- A repeated 7-second slot rhythm could become perceptible. Vary lead, action-beat timing, duration and rare empty-slot placement; then judge a continuous playback video. **Do not pretend internal slot jitter guarantees non-rhythmic pacing**. If the regular pulse remains obvious, investigate independently addressable chunk schedules with variable durations and bounded local lookup, rather than silently abandoning arbitrary seek.

For any simulation time `t >= 0`:
```text
slotIndex = floor(t / S);
localTime = t - slotIndex * S;
slotSeed = hash(dreamSeed, slotIndex);
spec = generateSlotSpec(dreamSeed, slotIndex);    // pure; independent of past runtime
visible = spec.hasEvent && lead <= localTime &&
          localTime < lead + duration;
activeEventCount = visible ? 1 : 0;
```

- `seek(t)` directly computes slot index in O(1) for arbitrary time; no replay from event zero.
- `seekEvent(n)` lands at **the reveal onset** of the n-th slot's apparition; for an empty slot, either return an explicit `emptySlot` result or expose `seekSlot(n)` instead. V1 specifies `seekSlot(n)` at slot boundary and `seekEvent(n)` at reveal if present, with empty-slot status otherwise; neither scans preceding slots.
- Pause or hidden tab freezes existing simulation time; resume must not attempt to catch up all missed slots.
- `New Dream` changes the dream seed and resets the local sequence. No persistent forest, dust or second event subsystem is permitted.

**Exactly one** refers to all event renderable objects together. An apparition morphing into multiple sparks remains one lifecycle owned by one event, but it may not spawn independent lasting companions. Black gaps must contain only the persistent walker on black.


## Perceived novelty engine

V1 must **not use a filter over the last N accepted events**: recursive acceptance checks can require re-evaluating every predecessor back to event zero, making `seek(t)` potentially O(n) or worse. A bounded window of *accepted* events is not automatically bounded dependency.

Instead, construct an **independently reproducible, fixed-size chunk schedule**:
- Partition slots into chunks of e.g. 12 slots. Use `hash(dreamSeed, chunkIndex)` to choose a shuffled/permuted, balanced sequence of morphology families and gesture motifs; each chunk is generated from only its own seed and index. Use a seed-derived permutation with no mutation across chunk boundaries.
- For slot `n`, look up `chunkIndex=floor(n/12)`, `withinChunk=n%12`; at most 12 small slot descriptors are constructed, **independent of n**. Determinism is preserved for direct seeks and replays, with no prior accepted-history recursion.
- Inside each chunk, optionally compare coarse feature signatures of *candidate specs in that same chunk* to reduce obvious immediate duplicates; use a fixed retry cap and stable tie-breaker. Never depend on previously accepted chunks.
- Because chunk boundaries can still accidentally repeat a family, optionally derive a boundary guard from two **independently generated raw chunk schedules**, selecting a deterministic tie-break candidate. No recursive appeal to the previous accepted choice.
- Perceptual novelty features: topology family and subvariant, shape occupancy/asymmetry, palette, action, relationship beat (gaze / imitation / retreat), trajectory and timing. A color change alone scores poorly.

This yields an unbounded stream, not infinite guaranteed *meaningful* originality. Evaluate contact sheets and real-time emotional legibility instead of claiming every frame contains a new species.

The generator may introduce new grammar constructors in future versions **without runtime LLM calls**. A versioned grammar seed/schema preserves reproducibility for already published builds; document when a grammar change intentionally changes the event stream.

## Rendering and budget

Use the current Canvas 2D and existing walk atlas. No ray tracing, WebGL or network inference in v1.

- **Event generation**: once when its slot becomes relevant; generate shape graph, action timeline and path from seeded PRNG. Optionally precompute the next event offscreen during the preceding black interval, within a small fixed work budget.
- **Chalk artwork**: prepare 2–3 deterministic line-boil variants per active event at canonical 12 Hz phase times; rotate/interpolate variants as an artistic choice, not regenerate every RAF. Avoid unbounded `(eventIndex, absolutePhase)` caches.
- **Motion**: smooth RAF transforms, blinking, appearance and fade computed from absolute simulation time. Keep 12 Hz texture boil separate from presentation cadence.
- **Glow**: tiny cached stroke halos or a limited number of extra contour strokes. No full-screen blur, full-screen readback, or per-frame noise generation.
- **Memory**: retain at most current event + one prefetched event, with bounded reusable canvases. Invalidate size-dependent rasters on resize; logical event identity stays stable.
- **Performance**: benchmark on target phone with fixed seed, viewport, DPR and tier. Use the numeric frame-delivery and cache checks from `tasks/moonlight_fog_research.md` as a starting benchmark, then set event-specific limits based on measurements. Never claim phone acceptance from mocked or desktop-only tests.

## Validation and acceptance

**Invariants**
- Across sampled times and immediately at every reveal/disappearance boundary, `activeEventCount <= 1` including glow, chalk particles, fragments and trails. Empty slots contain only the walker against true black.
- Same seed/slot/time/viewport/tier yields identical event ID, specification and raster after direct seek, stepped playback, repeat within-phase seeks, and pause/resume in the same browser.
- Independent chunk permutation schedule supports bounded lookup: generating slot 5 and slot 1,000,005 consumes comparable work. `seek(0)`, `seek(120)`, `seek(3600)`, `seek(1e6)` do not replay prior slots.
- Unit-test slot inequalities `lead+duration+tail<=S`, `tail>=1.15s`, event lifecycle/particle death before silence, and `seekEvent(n)` semantics for occupied and empty slots.
- Track cache/object counts over time and at event boundaries; no growth proportional to event index. Confirm 12 Hz drawing variants are independent of RAF history and do not cause unwanted world-motion stutter.
- Verify offline page with no runtime HTTP requests and preserve exactly the existing 33 walking frames.

**First artistic proof before scale testing**
- Produce **roughly 60 seconds** of real-time animation with **about 8–10 consecutive encounters** spanning all three families, including at least one micro-story directly responding to the walker.
- Human art-direction review must check silhouette beauty and readability, expressive movement, chalk texture, contrast, phone composition and the felt rhythm of **short gaps, occasional long silences and possible seven-second periodicity**.
- Reject disconnected random limb soup, obvious cut-and-paste stickers, events that cover the model, and scenes whose sole novelty is a palette switch. At least one representative screenshot/clip per family and one full-minute capture should accompany the initial implementation PR.

**Later breadth/performance acceptance, only after the first minute looks good**
- Generate **200 consecutive event** contact sheets to assess variation and the amount of visual sameness. Review at least 30 at real playback speed on a phone.
- Perform a blind comparison against five fixed motifs with randomized colors/scales. **Proposed, unverified** target: >=80% of inspected events judged visually distinct from immediate neighbors, without sacrificing legibility.
- Check 320x568, 390x844, 844x390 and desktop; record warmed physical-phone p95/p99 RAF intervals, 12 Hz update spikes, sustained memory and 10-minute continuous playback before marking phone acceptance.


## Suggested milestone

**Proof 1 — aesthetic minute:** In a new feature branch from current main, build an isolated black-stage experiment with the original walker and **three carefully constructed families** (winged apparition, watchful/eyed presence, abstract ribbon), each with constrained silhouette grammar and a micro-story connected to the walker. One slot owns one complete event, surrounded by black. Use a fixed-tier offline Canvas 2D build. Review **about 8–10 encounters in one minute** for expressiveness and pacing *before* doing a massive variability study.

**Proof 2 — novelty:** Once the drawings look worth watching, generate 200 indexed events and review a contact sheet plus real-time play, iterating grammar and deterministic 12-slot permutations. Add new families only if actual repetition or illegible forms justify it.

**Proof 3 — delivery:** Long seek/cache tests, phone warm-up/FPS checks and a dedicated GitHub Pages candidate under a unique non-root path. Do not overwrite the accepted root scene.

This PR remains concept-only; none of these implementations or phone measurements have yet occurred.

## Explicit non-goals

- Do not render a permanent forest, fog, moon, decorative border, or audience.
- Do not stack events, even if they are of different types.
- Do not call an LLM to invent a creature at runtime.
- Do not promise mathematically guaranteed perpetual semantic uniqueness from finite code.
- Do not publish over the existing GitHub Pages root preview.
